Statement

I am an architect based in Belgrade, Serbia, working across architecture, landscape, mapping, research, and ecological approaches to design.

I received my master’s degree in Architecture in Belgrade, after completing my bachelor’s degree in Russia. Over the past six years, I have worked with Russian, Serbian, and Arab companies, contributing to projects in residential architecture, landscape design, urban improvement, and public space development. Alongside my professional practice, I have participated in several international architectural competitions, including one winning proposal.

In the last two years, my work has focused mainly on concept development for parks in Abu Dhabi. This experience strengthened my interest in landscape architecture, territorial research, cartography, and design processes that begin with careful observation rather than with a fixed form.

A significant part of my practice is research-based. I use architectural tools not only to represent space, but also to investigate it: to measure, compare, map, model, and reveal patterns that are not immediately visible. I am interested in turning complex spatial questions into methods — whether through drawings, maps, data, diagrams, computational workflows, or code. I enjoy figuring out how something can be calculated, structured, visualized, and translated into a clear spatial argument.

Over time, my understanding of architecture has moved away from a purely corporate or linear career path. I am increasingly interested in architecture as a way of reading territories, producing knowledge, and imagining possible futures. For me, design is not only about creating objects or images, but about understanding the ecological, cultural, political, and everyday systems that shape space.

My practice combines concept development, spatial research, visual storytelling, project coordination, and site-specific strategies. I am interested in projects that connect architecture with landscape, environmental thinking, public space, computational research, and alternative ways of seeing and representing territory.

This position is not always the most predictable or secure professional path, but it is a chosen one. It allows me to work with architecture as a field of research, care, observation, calculation, and collaboration — a way to ask what spaces are, whom they serve, how they operate, and what else they could become.
